# Operating Principles

These principles guide how you use this system and make decisions. Edit them to reflect your actual beliefs.

---

## 1. Clarity over activity

Busy is not the same as effective. Being in motion is not the same as making progress.

**Implication**: If you can't explain why something matters, stop doing it.

---

## 2. Energy is the constraint, not time

You have the same 168 hours as everyone else. What differentiates high performers is how they manage their energy — physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual.

**Implication**: Optimize your calendar for energy, not efficiency. Protect your peak hours. Say no to energy vampires.

---

## 3. Strategy is about what you don't do

Every yes is a thousand nos. The best strategic decision is often to stop something, not start something.

**Implication**: Review what you're currently doing before adding anything new.

---

## 4. Patterns reveal more than events

A single bad day means nothing. A recurring pattern of bad days means everything.

**Implication**: Track enough to see patterns. This system exists to help you see what you can't see in real-time.

---

## 5. Regret minimization over short-term comfort

When you're 80, will you regret not trying this? Or will you regret wasting time on it?

**Implication**: Make hard decisions based on long-term regret, not short-term fear.

---

## 6. Honesty without self-flagellation

Reflection should be honest, not brutal. Acknowledge mistakes without shame. Recognize wins without arrogance.

**Implication**: This system is a mirror, not a judge.

---

## 7. Systems over willpower

Discipline fades. Systems endure. If something important keeps getting skipped, don't try harder — redesign the system.

**Implication**: If this system stops working for you, change it. Don't guilt yourself into compliance.

---

## 8. Leverage over effort

Working harder is rarely the answer. The question is: what creates disproportionate outcomes?

**Implication**: Measure results per unit of energy, not hours worked.

---

## 9. Alignment before optimization

If you're climbing the wrong mountain, it doesn't matter how fast you're moving.

**Implication**: Quarterly and annual reviews exist to check whether you're on the right mountain.

---

## 10. This system serves you, not the other way around

If any part of this system creates guilt, obligation, or performance anxiety, change it or delete it.

**Implication**: You are not failing the system. The system is failing you. Fix it.
