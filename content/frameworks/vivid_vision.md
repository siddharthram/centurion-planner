# Vivid Vision Framework

**Inspired by Tony Robbins and Cameron Herold's approach to future visioning.**

---

## What This Framework Does

A Vivid Vision is a written narrative of your future as if it's already happening. It's not a goal list — it's a story.

You write in **present tense**, describing a specific day in your life 3-5 years from now in rich, sensory detail. The idea is to make the future so tangible that your brain treats it as real, which influences your decisions today.

---

## When to Use It

- When setting long-term direction (3-5 year horizon)
- When you feel stuck or unclear about where you're going
- Once a year during your annual review
- Before making a major life decision

---

## How It Works

You write **1-3 pages** answering this question:

**"It's [DATE 3-5 years from now]. You're living your ideal life. Describe a single day from start to finish."**

Be specific. Use sensory details. Write as if it's already true.

### What to Include

- **Morning routine**: How do you wake up? What's the first thing you do?
- **Work or purpose**: What does your day look like? What are you working on or contributing to?
- **Energy**: How do you feel throughout the day?
- **Relationships**: Who are you spending time with? How do those interactions feel?
- **Environment**: Where do you live? What does your home or workspace look like?
- **Finances**: What does your financial situation enable? What are you not worried about?
- **Health**: What does your body feel like? What are your habits?
- **Meaning**: What are you contributing? What are you known for?
- **Evening**: How do you end the day? How do you feel as you go to sleep?

---

## Why This Framework Works

1. **Your brain can't distinguish vivid imagination from reality**: Neuroscience shows that visualizing a future state activates the same brain regions as experiencing it.
2. **It influences daily decisions**: When you have a clear vision, small choices become easier. "Does this move me toward the vision or away from it?"
3. **It's motivating**: Goal lists are abstract. A story is emotional and concrete.
4. **It reveals misalignment**: If you can't imagine your current path leading to the vision, that's critical data.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Being too vague**: "I'm successful and happy" is useless. "I wake up at 7am in my apartment downtown, do yoga for 30 minutes, then have a slow breakfast while reading" is vivid.
- **Writing what you think you should want**: This is private. Write what you actually want.
- **Ignoring constraints**: It's okay to be ambitious, but stay within the realm of achievable.
- **Only focusing on one area**: Your life is not just your career. Include all dimensions.

## Example Snippet

> It's June 15, 2029. I wake up at 7:00am feeling rested. Light is streaming through the windows of our home. I make coffee and sit quietly for 20 minutes — no phone, no notifications. Just coffee and presence.
>
> By 8:00am, the kids are up. We make breakfast together and talk about the day ahead. I'm not rushed. I feel calm. I drive them to school with music playing, and we sing along.
>
> At 9:30am, I settle into my work. I've built a schedule that works for my energy — deep focus in the morning, meetings in the afternoon. I'm doing work that challenges me and feels meaningful. I'm respected for my expertise in my field.
>
> At 3pm, I take a break and go for a walk. I've learned that movement clears my mind better than anything. I'm in the best shape I've been in years because I've made health a non-negotiable.
>
> At 4:30pm, I pick up the kids. We spend the late afternoon together — homework, playing, talking. I'm present, not distracted by my phone.
>
> Dinner is at 6pm with my family. We eat together and talk about our days. After the kids are in bed, I read for an hour. My partner and I catch up, really connecting.
>
> I go to bed by 10pm, feeling grateful. My life feels balanced. I'm not perfect, but I'm intentional. I'm building a life that reflects what actually matters to me.

---

## How to Write Yours

1. Set a timer for 45 minutes
2. Pick a date 3-5 years from now
3. Write a narrative of a single day in present tense
4. Don't edit as you go — just write
5. When the timer ends, read it back
6. Edit for clarity and vividness (add sensory details)

Save it in this file or in `goals/3_year.md`.

---

## How to Use It

- **Read it quarterly**: During your quarterly review, revisit your Vivid Vision. Are you on track?
- **Use it to make decisions**: When faced with a choice, ask: "Which option moves me closer to this vision?"
- **Update it as needed**: If the vision no longer excites you, rewrite it. You're allowed to change your mind.
