# Life Map Framework

**Adapted from Alex Lieberman's Life Map: six dimensions of a well-lived life.**

---

## What This Framework Does

Most people optimize one or two areas of life (usually career and finances) while ignoring the rest. Then they wonder why they feel unfulfilled despite "success."

The Life Map is a simple visual tool that scores six dimensions of your life:

1. **Career**: Your work, business, and professional growth
2. **Relationships**: Family, friendships, romantic partnership
3. **Health**: Physical fitness, energy, sleep, nutrition
4. **Meaning**: Purpose, contribution, legacy, spirituality
5. **Finances**: Wealth, savings, financial security
6. **Fun**: Hobbies, play, joy, experiences

You rate each dimension from 1-10, visualize the results, and identify where you're thriving vs. neglecting.

---

## When to Use It

- **Quarterly**: Quick check-in to see if you're balanced or lopsided
- **Annually**: Deep review during your annual reflection
- **When making major decisions**: If you're considering a new role, exit, relocation, or life change, the Life Map shows what you'll gain and lose

---

## How It Works

For each of the six dimensions, you'll:

1. **Rate your current state** (1-10)
2. **Define your target state** (1-10)
3. **Identify the gap**
4. **Decide what to do about it**

## How to Score

Use this rubric:

- **1-3**: This area is broken or severely neglected
- **4-6**: It's functional but not thriving
- **7-8**: It's good — you're satisfied
- **9-10**: It's exceptional — you wouldn't change a thing

Be honest. A "perfect 10" in every category is neither realistic nor necessary.

---

## Visual Representation

Once you've scored each dimension, you can visualize it as a radar chart (hexagon). You can sketch this on paper or use a tool like Excel/Google Sheets.

```
       Career (8)
            /\
           /  \
   Fun (4) ---- Relationships (6)
          |    |
Finances (9) - Meaning (5)
           \  /
            \/
        Health (3)
```

In this example:
- **Career** and **Finances** are strong
- **Health** is severely neglected
- **Fun** and **Meaning** need attention

## What to Do With This Information

### Step 1: Identify Imbalances
Where are the biggest gaps between your current state and your target?

### Step 2: Ask Why
Why is this dimension low? Is it:
- **Lack of time**: You're overinvested elsewhere
- **Lack of clarity**: You don't know what "good" looks like in this area
- **Lack of priority**: It's not actually important to you right now
- **External constraint**: Something outside your control (e.g., health issue, family crisis)

### Step 3: Decide What to Change
You can't optimize all six dimensions at once. Pick **one or two** to focus on this quarter.

**Examples**:
- "I'm at a 3 in Health. I'll commit to working out 3x/week and sleeping 7+ hours."
- "I'm at a 4 in Relationships. I'll have dinner with my family 5x/week with no phones."
- "I'm at a 5 in Fun. I'll block one Saturday per month for a hobby I actually enjoy."

### Step 4: Accept Trade-Offs
Some seasons of life require imbalance. If you're in a high-growth startup phase, your Career might be a 9 and your Fun might be a 4. That's okay — as long as it's **intentional** and **temporary**.

The danger is **unintentional, permanent imbalance**.

---

## Common Patterns

### The "All Career, No Life" Pattern
Career: 9, Finances: 8, Everything Else: 3-5

**Risk**: Burnout, relationship breakdown, health crisis

**Fix**: Deliberately invest in one neglected dimension this quarter

---

### The "Coasting" Pattern
Everything is a 5-6

**Risk**: Nothing is broken, but nothing is thriving either

**Fix**: Pick one dimension and push it to an 8

---

### The "Misaligned Success" Pattern
Career: 9, Finances: 9, Meaning: 3

**Risk**: You're "successful" by external metrics but feel empty

**Fix**: Revisit your North Star and Vivid Vision — are you climbing the right mountain?
