# Ideal Life Costing Framework

**Adapted from Tim Ferriss's "Dreamlining" exercise in The 4-Hour Workweek.**

---

## What This Framework Does

Most people have no idea what their "ideal life" actually costs. They assume it's unaffordable, so they never try.

This framework forces you to:
1. Define what your ideal life looks like (not a fantasy — a real, specific life)
2. Calculate what it actually costs per month
3. Determine if you can already afford it (or how close you are)

The result is usually shocking: your ideal life costs less than you think, and you're closer than you realize.

---

## When to Use It

- When you're unsure if you can afford to make a life change
- When you're considering a major financial decision (exit, new role, lifestyle change)
- Once a year during your annual review
- When you feel trapped by "needing more money"

---

## How It Works

You'll define three versions of your life and calculate the monthly cost of each:

1. **Minimum Viable Life**: The simplest version of a good life (not poverty — just minimal and intentional)
2. **Comfortable Life**: Your ideal day-to-day without excess
3. **Abundant Life**: The version with everything you want and no compromises

For each version, you'll list specific costs and add them up.

---

## Why This Framework Works

1. **It replaces vague anxiety with concrete numbers**: "I need to make more money" becomes "I need $12,000/month to live my ideal life, and I currently make $15,000/month."
2. **It reveals what you actually value**: If your "abundant life" doesn't include a yacht, you don't actually want a yacht.
3. **It exposes The Gap**: Most people discover they're already close to affording their ideal life — they're just spending on the wrong things.
4. **It informs decisions**: Should you sell the company? Stay in your job? Move cities? This framework gives you the financial clarity to decide.

---

## How to Calculate

For each category below, estimate the **monthly cost** of your ideal version.

Be specific. "A nice house" is useless. "A 2,500 sq ft house in Austin with a yard, costing $4,500/month" is useful.

### Categories

1. **Housing**: Rent or mortgage, utilities, maintenance
2. **Transportation**: Car payment, insurance, gas, or public transit
3. **Food**: Groceries, dining out, coffee
4. **Health**: Insurance, gym, supplements, therapy, medical
5. **Family**: Childcare, education, activities
6. **Travel**: Flights, hotels, experiences (annual cost ÷ 12)
7. **Entertainment**: Subscriptions, hobbies, fun
8. **Clothing**: Monthly average
9. **Savings/Investments**: Retirement, brokerage, emergency fund
10. **Giving**: Charitable contributions, helping family
11. **Miscellaneous**: Everything else

## The Analysis

Compare your current income to your three life versions:

- **If your current income > Comfortable Life cost**: You can already afford your ideal life. The question is: why aren't you living it?
- **If your current income is close**: You're probably one strategic decision away (better job, small raise, cutting unnecessary spending).
- **If there's a big gap**: You need a plan. What would it take to close the gap? New role? Exit? Different business model?

---

## What to Do With This Information

### Scenario 1: You Can Already Afford It
You're not money-constrained — you're decision-constrained. What's stopping you from living your ideal life? Fear? Inertia? Social pressure?

**Action**: Design a 90-day experiment. Test your "comfortable life" for three months and see how it feels.

---

### Scenario 2: You're Close
You need a 10-30% increase in income, or a 10-30% decrease in spending.

**Action**: Identify the fastest path to close the gap. Negotiate a raise? Cut unnecessary expenses? Launch a side project?

---

### Scenario 3: There's a Big Gap
You need a structural change — new career, exit, relocation, business pivot.

**Action**: Break the gap into milestones. What would it take to get to "Minimum Viable Life"? Then "Comfortable"? Then "Abundant"?

---

## Common Realizations

- "My ideal life costs $8,000/month, and I'm making $12,000/month. I'm already there — I just spend on things I don't care about."
- "I thought I needed $50,000/month to be happy. Turns out I need $15,000/month. The business exit I've been chasing isn't necessary."
- "I'm living the Minimum Viable Life but hating it. I need to earn more, not cut more."
- "My Abundant Life costs $25,000/month. I make $10,000/month. I need a 5-year plan to close this gap."
