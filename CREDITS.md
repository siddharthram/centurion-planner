# Credits & Inspirations

Centurion Planner is built on the shoulders of giants. These frameworks, methodologies, and concepts have been adapted and combined to create a cohesive personal operating system.

---

## Frameworks & Methodologies

### Life Map Framework
**Creator**: Alex Lieberman (Morning Brew co-founder)

The six dimensions of a well-lived life:
- Career / Work
- Relationships
- Health
- Meaning / Purpose
- Finances
- Fun / Joy

We use this framework to ensure goals and reviews cover all aspects of life, not just work.

---

### Vivid Vision
**Creators**: Tony Robbins & Cameron Herold

A narrative approach to envisioning your future as if it's already happening. Write in present tense, be specific, engage all senses.

Cameron Herold's book *Vivid Vision* and Tony Robbins' visualization techniques inform our long-term goal setting.

---

### Annual Review Process
**Creator**: Dr. Anthony Gustin

A question-based approach to annual reflection that surfaces patterns, blind spots, and insights. Emphasis on honest self-assessment rather than just metric tracking.

---

### Dreamlining / Ideal Life Costing
**Creator**: Tim Ferriss

From *The 4-Hour Workweek*: Calculate what your ideal life actually costs, break it into achievable targets, and work backward to make it happen.

---

### Regret Minimization Framework
**Creator**: Jeff Bezos

Ask yourself: "When I'm 80, will I regret not trying this?" Used in our 10-year goals template to test if your vision is worth pursuing.

---

## Gamification Inspiration

### Streak System
**Inspired by**: Duolingo

The streak mechanic (consecutive days of activity) is one of the most effective behavioral motivators. Duolingo pioneered making streaks feel valuable and losing them feel painful (in a motivating way).

### Points & Levels
**Inspired by**: Various productivity and fitness apps

The XP system, level progression, and achievement badges draw from gamification best practices established by apps like Duolingo, Headspace, and fitness trackers.

## Philosophy

### Personal Operating System Concept
The idea of treating your life like an operating system — with regular maintenance, reviews, and updates — draws from:
- **GTD (Getting Things Done)** by David Allen
- **The Bullet Journal Method** by Ryder Carroll
- **Atomic Habits** by James Clear
- **Essentialism** by Greg McKeown
